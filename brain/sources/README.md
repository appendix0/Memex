# sources

**Goes here:** Anything someone else wrote. Papers, API docs, archived
snapshots, third-party material. `type: source`.

**Does NOT go here:** Your own synthesis of them (`concepts/`). Your own results
(`research/`).

Every Book here carries `kind:` — see [[CLASSIFICATION]].

- **`kind: reference`** — documentation, a technique writeup, an API.
- **`kind: paper`** — a published work, kept with its original and with what the
  owner and the agent concluded about it. The rules below govern these.

## What a paper's Book holds

> "Trails become shareable intellectual infrastructure."
> — Bush, §8

The shelf exists to be *"a shelf that saves the original paper and my and my
agent's comments on it."*

1. **The abstract of the facts** — what the paper establishes, in the Library's
   own voice. Above the line, rewritten as understanding changes.
2. **The original**, verbatim, or an exact pointer to where it lives. Never a
   summary standing in for a source. **A summary alone cannot be checked against
   the source.**
3. **Comments, attributed and dated** — the owner's and the agent's, kept
   **separate** and **append-only**. A wrong reading stays visible; it is not
   corrected in place, it is answered by a later comment.
4. **The appendix** — `## Trail`, `## Timeline`, per [[schema]].

### Rules

- A comment is never edited or deleted. Wrong readings are evidence of how the
  reading changed, and that is exactly the §7.5 material this shelf keeps.
- **Reading a paper is not agreement.** A paper lays a step on a trail only
  under the both-agree rule.
- The original is not paraphrased into the facts. Keep both; they answer
  different questions.
