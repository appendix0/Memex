# As We May Think — Vannevar Bush, 1945

Bush's essay in *The Atlantic*, July 1945, describing the **memex**: a personal
device that stores a scholar's books, records and communications and, crucially,
lets its owner build **named associative trails** through them. It is the
document this system is named for and built against, and every design rule here
is traceable to a numbered section of it.

**The original is not reproduced here.** The essay is widely available from *The
Atlantic*'s own archive and from university course pages; keep an exact pointer
to the copy you actually read, with the date you read it. A summary alone cannot
be checked against its source, which is the whole reason this shelf keeps both.

## The abstract of the facts

- **§2 — the store is profligate; the record "must be continuously extended."**
  Bush wants everything kept. This is why session transcripts are retained
  whole, outside the Library, and why the Library itself is not where capture
  happens.
- **§3-4 — the machine does the repetitive part; judgment stays human.** The
  division that justifies a closed list of triggers rather than an agent's taste
  deciding what is worth recording.
- **§5 — "the difficulty seems to be… that we have to select from the record by
  indexing."** Facts without a tool that reaches them are not usable. This is
  why an unfindable Book counts as a failure, not as a filing success.
- **§6 — ordinary indexing does not disappear.** The memex keeps normal lookup
  and puts association *on top of* it. Classification and facets are that
  layer; trails are the layer above.
- **§7 — "The process of tying two items together is the important thing."**
  The trail, and the **button** the researcher presses to make one. Nothing ties
  itself. This is the single sentence the whole write path is built on.
- **§7.6 — a trail earns its place by the question not yet asked.** Bush's
  Turkish-bow trail was built to answer "why was the bow better than the
  crossbow?" and reused years later for "why do societies resist better
  technology?" A record is worth keeping when it will answer a question nobody
  has asked yet.
- **§7.7 — trails transfer.** A trail must hold for a reader who was not in the
  room, which is why every step names its reason rather than assuming context.
- **§8 — "trails can become shared knowledge artifacts."** Sharing is a property
  of every trail, not a separate tier above them.

## What this system takes from it, and what it does not

**Takes:** the four operations (search, index, link, trail); the store/trail
split; the button as a deliberate human press; profligacy about the store and
strictness about the abstract.

**Does not take:** the microfilm, the dry photography, the head-mounted camera,
and the assumption that the *owner* is the only thing that can write. That last
one is the real departure — Bush's memex could not author anything on its own,
so he never had to say "nothing enters except through the button". Here the
agent can write, so the rule has to be stated and enforced in code.
